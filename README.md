# From Pixels to Patients

**A guided tour of attribution methods on lung CT.**

📊 **[Open the interactive tour →](https://bardli.github.io/pixels-to-patients/)**

Seven attribution methods, each shown as the real function that produced its
heat-map, measured against a **published, third-party medical model** rather
than a toy CNN, on real lung-CT nodule crops. Every heat-map, number and figure on the site is generated by the
code in this repository and hashed in a provenance manifest. Nothing is drawn by
hand.

- **Model** — [JSC](https://github.com/bowang-lab/JSC) (bowang-lab), the FLARE26
  AutoMSC joint segmentation + classification baseline: an nnU-Net
  `PlainConvUNet` encoder with an FPN classification head, at the published
  fold-3 checkpoint (validation AUROC **0.8875**).
- **Data** — FLARE-AutoMSC `Dataset005_LUNA25`: 6132 screening-CT nodule crops
  labelled for malignancy.
- **Methods** — mean activation (baseline) · Grad-CAM · Guided Grad-CAM ·
  LayerCAM · Occlusion sensitivity · Integrated Gradients · Integrated Grad-CAM

---

## Why this exists

Built for the **MICCAI Educational Challenge 2026** (theme: *From Pixels to
Patients*).

Attribution tutorials almost always demonstrate on 2D natural images and a
small CNN the author trained themselves. That setting hides the three things
that bite in medical imaging:

1. **3D, anisotropic volumes.** A CAM tap in a 3D U-Net encoder sits at a
   different resolution along each axis. Here the tap is `640 × 16 × 16 × 16`
   for a `64 × 128 × 128` patch: ÷4 in z, ÷8 in-plane, because the CT is
   2.0 mm through-plane and 0.66 mm in-plane.
2. **A model you did not train.** You inherit its head shape, its activation
   functions, its normalisation, and its bugs. JSC's binary head emits a
   *single* logit, which quietly breaks the standard `logits[:, class_idx]`
   indexing that most attribution libraries assume.
3. **A lesion that is present in both classes.** LUNA25 asks *malignant or
   benign*, and every case has a nodule. "Did the heat land on the lesion?" is
   therefore necessary but **not sufficient**. The model has to read the
   nodule's texture, not merely find it. That is a sharper test than the usual
   presence/absence framing.

The tour walks one real case end to end, through the encoder to the tap to each
method's map, then scores every method against the radiologist's mask.

## What it shows

Averaged over five exemplar cases chosen to cover all four outcomes
(2 true-positive, 1 true-negative, 1 false-positive, 1 false-negative):

| Method | enrichment ↑ | pointing ↑ |
|---|---|---|
| Grad-CAM | **5.10×** | 0.20 |
| Occlusion sensitivity | 5.05× | 0.20 |
| LayerCAM | 4.63× | 0.20 |
| Integrated Grad-CAM | 4.15× | 0.40 |
| Mean activation *(baseline)* | 4.08× | 0.40 |
| Integrated Gradients | 2.66× | 0.00 |
| Guided Grad-CAM | 1.16× | **1.00** |

`enrichment` is how much more concentrated a heat-map is on the nodule than a
uniform map would be. **1.00× means no better than uniform.** It replaces raw
"mass in ground truth", which does not survive real lesions: across these five
cases the nodule occupies anywhere from **0.006% to 2.16%** of the patch (58 to
22,686 voxels, a 391× spread), so raw mass collapses to 0.007–0.031 *and* stops
being comparable between cases: the 58-voxel lesion cannot reach the
22,686-voxel one's mass at equal attribution quality. Dividing out the volume
fraction fixes both: a uniform map scores exactly 1.00× whatever the lesion size.

Two findings the site is built around:

- **The metrics disagree.** Guided Grad-CAM finds the single hottest
  voxel inside the nodule on *every* case (`pointing 1.00`) while enriching only
  1.16× — its heat is essentially uniform apart from that one peak. Grad-CAM is
  the reverse. There is no single "best" method; there is only the question you
  asked.
- **Averaging is a weak localiser, and the reason is measurable.** Only 1 of the
  640 channels at the tap is exactly silent, but the channel means are bimodal
  across two orders of magnitude, and the lower half of the channels contributes
  **0.7%** of the average. The class-specific signal gets divided by 640.

Because JSC also segments, its own predicted mask gives a second, independent
read on where the model thinks the lesion is, so attribution faithfulness can
be cross-checked against the model's explicit output, not just against the
ground truth.

## Reproducing it

### Before you start — two walls

**The imaging data is gated and approval is manual.** `FLARE-AutoMSC` on Hugging
Face requires you to request access and wait for the organisers to grant it. Do
this first. The model weights are *not* gated and can be fetched immediately.

**The pipeline was built and measured on 3× RTX 6000 Ada (CUDA).** Every script
defaults to `--device cuda`. `--device cpu` works but a full-patch occlusion
sweep over 1575 positions takes tens of minutes per case on CPU.

### 1. Environment

```bash
git clone https://github.com/Bardli/pixels-to-patients.git
cd pixels-to-patients
uv sync
```

JSC is an nnU-Net v2.6.2 fork and needs two local patches before it installs —
its packaging and its dependency list are both incomplete upstream. The exact
commands, the pinned commit, and SHA-256 digests for every external artifact are
in **[`docs/jsc-luna25-sources.md`](docs/jsc-luna25-sources.md)**.

### 2. Model weights (not gated)

Fetch the published fold-3 checkpoint into the layout nnU-Net's predictor
expects: the *trainer* directory, with `dataset.json` and `plans.json` beside a
`fold_3/` folder:

```bash
B=https://huggingface.co/cyyu96/AutoMSC-Baselines/resolve/main/Dataset005_LUNA25/nnUNetCLSTrainerMTL__nnUNetPlans__3d_fullres
M=artifacts/jsc/model/nnUNetCLSTrainerMTL__nnUNetPlans__3d_fullres
mkdir -p "$M/fold_3"
for f in plans.json dataset.json dataset_fingerprint.json; do
  curl -sSL --fail -o "$M/$f" "$B/$f"
done
curl -sSL --fail -o "$M/fold_3/checkpoint_best.pth" "$B/fold_3/checkpoint_best.pth"
```

Expect `checkpoint_best.pth` to be **443,676,001 bytes**, SHA-256
`33ad8109aafd5f0b6d6c82a7f6911fa4167c36e44c9c4e50bed3c19aef25968f`.

### 3. Imaging data (gated)

Log in, then fetch the split metadata and the fold-3 validation cases:

```bash
huggingface-cli login          # or export HF_TOKEN=...

D=https://huggingface.co/datasets/FLARE-MedFM/FLARE-AutoMSC/resolve/main/Dataset005_LUNA25
mkdir -p artifacts/jsc/luna25_meta
for f in splits_final.json cls_data.csv dataset.json; do
  curl -sSL --fail -H "Authorization: Bearer $(cat ~/.cache/huggingface/token)" \
    -o "artifacts/jsc/luna25_meta/$f" "$D/$f"
done

uv run python scripts/download_luna25_fold3.py --fold 3
```

The downloader is resumable, validates that every file is real gzip before
accepting it (a gated 401 returns a short HTML body that would otherwise sit on
disk masquerading as data), and re-scans the disk at the end rather than trusting
its own exit status.

Expect **2578 files, 1770 MB** (1289 image + label pairs) under `data/luna25/`,
and a per-file SHA-256 manifest at `artifacts/jsc/fold3_val_manifest.csv`.

### 4. Run the attribution

```bash
uv run python scripts/jsc_gradcam.py \
  --cases 203125_1_19990102 100438_5_20010102 \
  --methods notgradcam truegradcam guided_gradcam layercam \
            occlusion integrated_gradients integrated_gradcam
```

> **Naming gotcha.** Grad-CAM is `truegradcam` in the attribution module
> (`scripts/gradcam3d_viz.py`) but `gradcam` in the site bundle;
> `scripts/jsc_web_export.py` maps between them. Pass the *module* names here.

Prints one line per case with the input shape, ground truth, prediction and
outcome badge, and writes the figures to `artifacts/jsc/gradcam_out/`:

```
[jsc] network ready on cuda, TF32 off, TTA off, eval()
[1/2] 203125_1_19990102  input (1,64,128,128)  GT=1 Pred=1 -> TP
[2/2] 100438_5_20010102  input (1,64,128,128)  GT=0 Pred=0 -> TN
```

### 5. Export the site bundle and serve it

```bash
uv run python scripts/jsc_web_export.py \
  --cases 203125_1_19990102 207347_1_20010102 100438_5_20010102 \
          100471_3_20000102 134082_1_19990102

python -m http.server 8137 --directory web
```

Writes `web/public/data/`: `manifest.json`, `model_graph.json`,
`benchmark.json`, and one directory per case holding the CT slice, the ground
truth and predicted masks, and all seven heat-maps as paired PNG + JSON (the
PNGs are raw pixel data, one pixel per voxel; the JSON drives the hover
read-outs). Schema `gradcam-repro.web-bundle.v2`, frozen in
[`web/DATA_CONTRACT.md`](web/DATA_CONTRACT.md).

Then open <http://localhost:8137>. It must be served over HTTP. Opening
`index.html` over `file://` blocks the data `fetch()`.

## Declared deviations from official JSC inference

Your probabilities **will not match** the official `segcls_ensemble_infer.py`
exactly, by design. All four deviations are recorded in the bundle manifest:

| Deviation | Why |
|---|---|
| `eval()` rather than `train()` | The official script calls `.train()` inside `inference()`. The classification head contains `Dropout(0.5)`, so the same case scored twice gives different logits and different heat-maps. Attribution has to be deterministic. |
| Test-time augmentation off | Mirroring would place gradients in flipped frames. |
| No geometric resampling | LUNA25 ships pre-cropped to the model's exact `(64,128,128)` patch — verified for all 1289 fold-3 validation cases, one single distinct shape. Running the plans resample rescales the image and misaligns it against the on-disk mask (IoU 0.196) while losing in-plane resolution. Skipping it gives IoU 1.000. **The official script does resample**, so probabilities differ. |
| TF32 disabled | Reproducible arithmetic across runs. |

The full measurement record, including three bugs found in the official
inference script and two in the attribution module, is in
[`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-29-jsc-wiring-findings.md`](docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-29-jsc-wiring-findings.md).
